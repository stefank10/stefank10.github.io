AFL team analytics

The question
Which team stats reflect how a club genuinely plays, and what actually explains a match margin? Most stat talk mixes real signal with week-to-week noise.
What I built
A reproducible Python project on public AFL data from 2019 to 2026. It tests the data against official results, measures how repeatable each team stat is, models the margin (trained on 2019 to 2025, tested on the unseen 2026 season) and maps the style of all 18 clubs.
What it showed
The model cut the typical error from 25.3 points (inside 50s alone) to 19.3. Along the way I caught a model that looked too good: rebound 50s were rebuilding the scoreboard, so they came out and a test now stops it happening again. I chose the simpler model on purpose, because a coach has to be able to read it.
Tools
Python (pandas, scikit-learn, statsmodels), R (fitzRoy), pytest, Git, AI coding agent

Plays like: a player similarity finder

The question
When a recruiter needs "another one of him" for free agency, the trade period or an injury replacement, where do they start? Usually from memory and vision alone.
What I built
Profiles of every player-season since 2019 from 15 per-game rates, adjusted for time on ground. Clustering finds eight roles (rucks, key forwards, rebounding defenders and so on) from what players actually do, because public data doesn't record positions. For any 2026 player, the finder ranks the ten most similar players and lists the shared strengths behind each match.
What it showed
Roles hold up: players keep the same role from one season to the next 73% of the time, against 13% by chance. It's a starting point for a recruiting conversation, not a substitute for watching vision.
Tools
Python (pandas, scikit-learn), k-means clustering, cosine similarity, JavaScript

Development curves and a breakout watchlist

The question
At contract time, which young players are genuinely ahead of the usual path, and which just had one good year?
What I built
A contribution score for every player (their own stats weighted by the margin model's points per stat), then a mixed-effects model of how that score changes with age, comparing each player with himself. It's backtested on 2024, 2025 and 2026, each time trained only on earlier seasons.
What it showed
The typical player peaks at about 26. The model beat "same as last season" by about 5% for under-23s, but not in every year, so the watchlist is a prompt for a closer look rather than a verdict. Projections pull back towards typical, because one big season is partly luck.
Tools
Python (statsmodels mixed-effects models, pandas), backtesting

Automated match reports

The problem
Weekly match reports meant hours of exporting, copying and formatting before anyone could read a number, and the layout changed depending on who built it.
What I built
R Markdown reports that run end to end: data in, formatted report out, with a title page, contents, positional benchmarks, load quadrants and a plain-language coach summary. I built and maintain these at Geelong. The sample shown here is rebuilt on synthetic data.
Impact
One consistent report staff can scan in minutes.
Tools
R, R Markdown, ggplot2, Catapult OpenField exports

Joining up HP data

The problem
Athlete data lived in separate systems, so staff spent time exporting and merging spreadsheets before they could even ask a question, and nobody could see how the numbers got from one place to another.
What I built
Pipelines that bring Catapult, VALD and Smartabase data into Snowflake. R Shiny apps that give staff one place to view and enter data. In-house rebuilds of reports the department used to license. Simple process maps so staff can see how their data flows, and a questionnaire to find the next bottlenecks worth automating.
Impact
Fewer manual steps, lower licensing costs, and staff who trust the data because it isn't a black box.
Tools
R, R Shiny, Python, SQL, Snowflake, vendor APIs

What I do
I support elite clubs' live GPS and reporting on match day for Catapult Sports. I triage problems in real time, fix what I can on the spot and escalate only what genuinely needs a specialist. Before that I ran live GPS collection at Richmond's first-team training and matches.
